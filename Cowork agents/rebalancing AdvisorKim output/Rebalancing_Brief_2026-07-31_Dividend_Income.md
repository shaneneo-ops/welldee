## Market Context (relevant to income vehicles)

Singapore REIT ETFs currently carry headline yields of 5–6% (SORA has fallen sharply to ~1.3%, a genuine tailwind for S-REIT distributable income into FY2026–27), and SGX-listed distributions face no withholding tax for individuals — you receive the full stated yield. US-listed high-dividend equity ETFs (SCHD, VYM, HDV) currently yield 2.3–3.4% gross, but as a non-US-person you'd only net roughly 70% of that after the 30% NRA dividend withholding — SCHD's 3.36% heActional yield effectively becomes ~2.35% in your pocket. Covered-call income ETFs (JEPI 8.03%, JEPQ 10.84%) look dramatically higher, but a meaningful share of their distributions can be option-premium income or return-of-capital rather than ordinary dividends, and how that's characterized for a non-resident alien's withholding varies by fund and by year — not something to size a core income plan around without a cross-border tax check first.

| Instrument type | Trend | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| SGX S-REIT ETFs (CLR, BYI, etc.) | Favorable | Falling SORA lowers funding costs, lifting distributable income; zero withholding drag |
| US high-dividend equity ETFs (SCHD, VYM, HDV) | Neutral | Solid, liquid, well-diversified — but 30% withholding meaningfully cuts net yield for you |
| US covered-call ETFs (JEPI, JEPQ) | Uncertain | Very high headline yield, but tax character for NRAs is fund/year-specific — don't rely on the full headline number |
| Endowus Higher Income portfolio | Favorable, but structural | 5–6% target payout, native to your existing platform — but its 20/80 equity/FI mix would pull your overall allocation well below 80% equities if used at scale |

## Tax Implications

No capital gains tax applies to any of this — Singapore doesn't tax capital gains and you're not a US taxpayer, so switching Endowus sleeves or trimming/topping up SGX ETFs carries no CGT cost. The relevant lens is withholding tax on the income itself: SGX-listed distributions (CLR, BYI, MBH, A35, ES3, D05, C6L) reach you with **zero withholding** — the stated yield is the real yield. US-listed distributions (SCHD, VYM, HDV, JEPI, JEPQ) face the standard **30% NRA withholding** (no US–Singapore tax treaty exists, so there's no reduced treaty rate the way there is for Ireland-UCITS) — budget for roughly 70 cents on the dollar of stated yield. For JEPI/JEPQ specifically, part of the distribution may be characterized as option premium or return of capital rather than a dividend, which can change the withholding treatment year to year — worth a direct check with a cross-border tax advisor before sizing a position meaningfully.

**US estate tax note**: you already hold GLD.US (SGD 16,200, ~US$12,600 US-situs). Any new US-listed ETF (SCHD, JEPI, etc.) adds to your cumulative US-situs exposure against the ~US$60,000 non-resident-alien estate tax threshold. At the sizes discussed in Options A/B (a few thousand to ~SGD 10-15K in US-listed picks), you'd remain comfortably under the threshold — but worth tracking if you scale up US-listed income positions further.

**Endowus Income portfolio's payout target is a target, not a guarantee**: 5-6% (Higher Income) or 3.5-4.5% (Future Income) are stated targets based on underlying fund coupons/dividends, not contractual yields — they can move with rate cycles.
