## Tax Implications

You're Singapore tax-resident and not a US person — **Singapore has no capital gains tax**, so this is not a "harvest losses / realize gains" decision the way it would be for a US taxpayer, and it doesn't apply to Action 1 anyway since nothing is being sold. The relevant lens for future actions (like any eventual ES3.SG trim, which again triggers no CGT) is **dividend/interest withholding tax on foreign holdings**:

- **MBH.SG and A35.SG (this trade)**: Singapore-domiciled, SGD-denominated — no US withholding exposure at all. This is one of the reasons they're the recommended pick here.
- **If you ever consider US-listed bond ETFs (BND, IEF, etc.) instead**: US-source *interest* income paid to a non-US person is often exempt from the 30% withholding that applies to equity *dividends*, under the "portfolio interest exemption" (IRC §871/881) — many large bond funds designate distributions as exempt "interest-related dividends." In practice this doesn't always work cleanly through every broker, and — more importantly — **the ETF shares themselves remain shares of a US-domiciled fund, which is generally still treated as US-situs property for US estate tax purposes** regardless of what the fund holds underneath. You already hold GLD.US (SGD 16,225, itself US-situs), and adding a US-listed bond ETF position of the size contemplated here (~SGD 45,840) would push your cumulative US-situs holdings past the ~US$60,000 threshold where non-resident aliens' estates become subject to US estate tax exposure. This is a genuine area of tax nuance — **I'm not a tax advisor, and if you want to use US- or Ireland-domiciled bond ETFs for a future allocation, it's worth a short conversation with a cross-border tax advisor to confirm current treatment.** For this trade, the SGD-domiciled options sidestep the question entirely, which is part of why they're the primary recommendation.
